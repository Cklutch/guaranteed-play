  - **Verified as FALSE POSITIVES, not applied**: Najee Harris, Tank Dell, Jalen McMillan. All three matched the same "recent TD pace far above projection" pattern as Johnston/Wilson, but each has a real, current, serious injury (Achilles tear + new team; multi-ligament knee reconstruction, opens the season on IR; severe 2025 neck injury plus a fresh 2026 camp knee injury, respectively) that legitimately justifies a conservative projection. Confirms the scan's raw pattern-match has a real false-positive rate and every candidate needs individual verification, not batch application.
  - **Flagged, deliberately NOT applied** (reasoning kept inline in `dads_scoring.py` so it isn't lost): Josh Jacobs (real gap, but already has a separate missed-games correction from the Exempt List news -- composing two corrections risks double-counting, needs a deliberate revision of the existing one rather than a second independent pct); George Kittle (real gap, but 8 months out from a January 2026 Achilles tear -- legitimate ongoing injury risk, not a clean modeling error); Zach Charbonnet (mixed signal -- real opportunity increase from Kenneth Walker's departure, but Charbonnet himself opened 2026 on PUP from his own ACL tear, needs Jacobs-style treatment); Cam Skattebo (real positive signal, confirmed presumptive Giants RB1, but the underlying pace is from only 8 rookie games in a still-developing role before a season-ending injury -- thinner sample, lower confidence); Jacoby Brissett (real find, confirmed 2026 starter, but it's a single injury-vacancy season for a QB with a long backup history). ## 2026-08-30

- **Dad's League scoring now uses STANDARD-scoring ADP, not the board's Half-PPR ADP.** New source `data/raw/juicebox_2026_adp_sources_standard.csv` (user-supplied JuiceBoxOne blend), loaded by `dads_scoring.load_dads_adp()` and exposed as `dads_adp` / `dads_adp_avg`. Deliberately kept as a SEPARATE file from the existing Half-PPR cut (`juicebox_2026_adp_sources.csv`) that feeds the main board's own `adp` — merging them would silently drift the standard board's market signal toward a scoring format it isn't. Dad's league has no PPR, so a Half-PPR ADP systematically over-ranks high-target possession receivers relative to how a no-PPR room actually drafts. Real magnitude: 24 of the top 32 players get a different ADP between the two modes (Derrick Henry 18 → 10).
- **Per-player TD-length bonus** in `dads_points_from_stats()`. The length bonus was three flat position-wide constants (`PASS/RUSH/REC_TD_LEN_BONUS_EV`) — every RB got the same 0.6 whether he was a breakaway back or a goal-line plunger. Now tilted per player by projected yards-per-touchdown as an explosiveness proxy (no per-play TD-distance data exists locally; this is an honest proxy, not a measurement). Verified as real signal, not volume noise: ~2x spread within position (RB rush 88–183 yds/TD, WR rec 82–222, TE rec 77–184). Shrunk toward the position average below `TD_LENGTH_FULL_CREDIT_TDS` projected TDs and bounded to `[0.55, 1.70]` so it stays a tilt, not a new dominant term. Below `TD_LENGTH_MIN_SAMPLE` TDs the multiplier is exactly 1.0 — identical to old behavior. Omitting the `length_baselines` argument reproduces the old flat formula exactly (pinned by test).
- **Three integration gaps closed, all of which made the above invisible or wrong in the live app:**
  - `Home.py::_scoring_version()` did not watch `draftkit/dads_scoring.py`, `winwithodds_season_projections.csv`, or the new Standard ADP sheet. `add_dads_scores()` runs *inside* the cached `_rankings()`, so edits to dad's scoring rules or a dropped-in new ADP sheet left the board serving stale dad's scores until a full process restart — indistinguishable from the caching bug that function exists to prevent. All three added to the cache key.
  - `Home.py` swapped projection/VOR/score in Dad's mode but NOT `adp`, so the displayed ADP column and the "Engine vs ADP" delta compared a Standard-ADP-derived score against the Half-PPR market. Now swaps `adp` → `dads_adp` alongside the rest.
  - `live_draft.build_candidate_pool()` never carried `dads_adp` at all. This was the consequential one: `adp` is what the entire survival model (`draftkit/survival.py`) runs on, so Dad's League mode in the Draft Command Center was computing opportunity cost, landing odds, and turn-planner pairs from half-PPR draft behaviour. Now swaps the market column when `score_col == "dads_final_score"`.
- Added `draftkit/tests/test_dads_scoring.py` (8 tests) pinning the flat-behavior fallback, the low-sample floor, the shrinkage band, the multiplier bounds, and the explosive-vs-grinder ordering.

## 2026-08-28

- **Removed the QB/TE draft discipline entirely** (user-directed). Gone from `draftkit/live_draft.py`: `elite_available_for()`, `_apply_elite_position_discipline()`, and the four constants that drove them (`ELITE_POSITION_RANK=3`, `ELITE_HOLD_ROUND=7`, `NO_ELITE_HOLD_ROUND=9`, `POSITION_VALUE_MARGIN=15`). It used to filter QB and TE out of the candidate pool *before scoring* until round 7 — or round 9 if no top-3 option remained — unless the player had fallen to/past his ADP (or 15+ picks past it with no elite left). Dad's League was exempt. Effect of the removal: QB and TE are now ranked on the same terms as RB/WR (value, risk, need, scarcity, opportunity cost) with no positional gate. Verified behaviour change — at R2/pick 20 the slate now surfaces Brock Bowers, Josh Allen, and Trey McBride, all of which the hold made impossible; at 1.01 no QB/TE appears anyway, because on value alone they don't beat the elite RB/WRs. To restore, revert this commit — the logic is self-contained.
- `NEED_BONUS_OVERRIDES` went from `{"TE": 3.0, "QB": 4.0}` to `{}`. Those discounts existed *because* of the hold ("the whole strategy is to actively avoid filling either early"), so they went with it; an unfilled QB/TE starter slot is now worth the same 6.0 as an unfilled RB/WR one. Left as an empty dict so per-position tuning stays a one-line change.
- **Deliberately KEPT: `ROSTER_CAPS = {"QB": 1, "TE": 1}`.** This is a roster-shape rule (don't recommend a second QB in a 1-QB league), not a restriction on drafting one early, and it has a second, unrelated job — `survival.make_need_multiplier()` uses it to know an opponent who already has his QB is no threat to take another. Removing it would degrade the opponent-need model as a side effect.
- `recommend_picks()`'s `scoring_mode` and `current_round` parameters are now **inert** — their only consumer was the hold. Kept on the signature (every caller already passes them, and a future position rule would want them) but the docstring now says so rather than claiming they enforce a discipline that no longer exists.
- Added `draftkit/survival.py` — the "will he still be there?" model, extracted so `live_draft` and the new `turn_optimizer` can share it without an import cycle. Three views of one curve: unconditional (the board's gauge), conditional-on-available-now measured in *opponent* picks, and opponent-need-aware. The middle one fixes a real error at the turn, where the unconditional curve counted your own back-to-back pick as a chance to lose the player (~8% reported where the true answer is 100%). The third scales each intervening pick by whether the team on the clock still needs that position; it requires Sleeper's per-team attribution and falls back to plain ADP in Manual mode. Both reductions are pinned by `test_survival.py`.
- Added `draftkit/turn_optimizer.py` + `draft_center.render_turn_planner()` — best two-player *combination* for back-to-back picks (slots 1/12; 2/3 and 10/11 as near-turns).
- **Opportunity cost now measures REPLACEABILITY, not per-player scarcity.** Reported live: the board offered Justin Herbert (our_score 35.8, ~10% to return) as ★BEST PICK over Trevor Lawrence (40.4, the #1 QB left, ~92% to return) — "take the worse player because he's scarcer." That is wrong whenever the two are substitutes: you roster one QB, so Herbert's scarcity buys nothing when a better QB is near-certain to be there next pick. `survival_pts` is now driven by `replaceable(p) = 1 − Π(1 − survival(q))` over players at p's position with `our_score >= p's` (p included, so it can only exceed his own survival). Herbert falls from rank 1 to 28; Lawrence becomes the top QB. The split is need-dependent and that matters — replaceability applies only when **one** starter slot remains at the position (substitutes); with several open (RB/WR early) the players are **complements**, you want both, and the original per-player survival is kept so the scarce one is still taken first. An intermediate attempt applied a "k better players survive" tail everywhere, which lost the floor that a player is replaceable by himself: it zeroed the penalty on Jordan Addison at 97% to return and ranked him 2nd while penalising DK Metcalf (7% to return, same value) by −17.3. Both directions are now pinned by `draftkit/tests/test_opportunity_cost.py`.
- Added a `WAIT` model note ("A better QB is 100% to still be here next pick"). Without it the card contradicts itself — the survival gauge answers "will HE be here?" while the ranking answers "will someone as good be here?", which is how the confusion above surfaced in the first place. **Known inconsistency, not yet fixed:** the displayed gauge still uses the *unconditional* `survival_pct`, while the engine uses the conditional/need-aware model, so the two can quote different percentages for the same player.

- Rewired `ceiling_score` and `sportsbook_advantage_score` (2026-08-27) to reuse real, already-live signals instead of the dead `ceiling_projection`/`floor_projection`/`sportsbook_projection` columns:
  - `ceiling_score` now reuses the exact upside signal powering Home.py's live Ceiling view (`role_usage_td_score` + `volatility_diagnostic` from `risk_variables.csv`, plus an event/big-play archetype lift), merged in by player_name. First pass dropped Home.py's percentile-of-relevance damping as "a different job" -- that was wrong: undamped, it inverted the whole ranking (deep-bench/practice-squad names at the top, Gibbs/CMC/Chase at the bottom), exactly the "TD vulture" failure Home.py's own comment says the damping exists to prevent. Added the same damping back (`_ceiling_relevance`, a projection-points percentile computed vectorized before the per-row loop).
  - `sportsbook_advantage_score` now merges real data from `data/processed/sportsbook_vs_adp_comparison.csv` (300 real players, `sportsbook_half_ppr_points`) instead of the mock `sportsbook_provider.fetch_sportsbook_projection_data()` path (which only covers 3 hardcoded players). Wiring in real data exposed a second, separate, pre-existing bug in the scoring formula itself: `edge_pct = (sportsbook - projection) / projection` blows past the 100 clip ceiling for any player with a small projection baseline (backup RBs projected at 17-35 points swing 100%+ on a modest absolute point difference), the same class of bug CLAUDE.md documents Base Value's own market component was built to avoid. Fixed by switching to a percentile rank of the raw point-edge across the pool (mirroring Base Value's `adp_pct = (-adp).rank(pct=True)` pattern) instead of a per-player ratio.
  - **Follow-up fix, same day**: normalized the edge within `SPORTSBOOK_EDGE_TIER_COUNT` (6) ADP-quantile tiers (`pd.qcut` on `adp_rank`, pulled from the same `sportsbook_vs_adp_comparison.csv` row as the edge itself) instead of ranking across the whole pool -- "does the market like this player more than typical for someone drafted around here," not an absolute tier-conflated edge. Verified as a real, measurable improvement, not just a plausible-sounding change: Ja'Marr Chase's score moved 26.8 -> 52.1, Christian McCaffrey's 17.6 -> 39.6, both crossing to roughly neutral. Not a full fix, though, and this is an inherent limit of discrete tiers rather than a bug: the underlying trend is smoothly monotonic in ADP rank (round 1-2 mean edge -31.0, round 3-5 -22.3, round 6-10 -11.3, round 11+ -7.5), not a clean step function, so 6 buckets approximate it without fully flattening it -- a player near the top of a ~47-player tier can still sit slightly below tier-mates drafted a bit later in the same bucket. `sportsbook_advantage_score` is now the dominant driver of the top-15 (elevated in 15/15, correlation +0.29 with the final blended score) -- more so than `ceiling_score` (correlation -0.14, elevated in only 4/15). A finer-grained or regression-detrended (edge ~ adp_rank residuals) version would close more of the remaining gap if it matters enough to pursue further.
